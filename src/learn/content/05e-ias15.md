# IAS15

## Idea

Fit a high-order polynomial to the pull over each step, and shrink the step whenever the last term of the polynomial is not tiny.

## Analogy

A driver who slows down whenever the road gets twisty, and speeds up on the straights.

## Strengths and weaknesses

Fifteenth order and **adaptive**: it takes tiny steps through a close encounter and long ones through quiet stretches. Its error is close to the roundoff of the computer, and grows slowly. It is not symplectic and not exactly reversible, but its error is so small that this rarely matters. Its weakness is cost per step.

## Cost

Several force evaluations per step: about twenty in the runs here, and it varies.

## When to use it

Close encounters, chaotic systems, and as the reference answer for judging the others.

:::deeper
Over a step the acceleration is $\mathbf a(h)=\mathbf a_0+\sum_{k=0}^{6}\mathbf b_k h^{k+1}$ for $h\in[0,1]$, fixed by its values at seven Gauss–Radau nodes. The step is accepted when $\varepsilon=\max|b_6|/\max|\mathbf a|$ is below the tolerance, and the next step is $dt\,(\varepsilon_{tol}/\varepsilon)^{1/7}$. The error estimate reads roundoff as truncation error when two bodies are very close and far from the origin, which makes the step collapse: that is a known limit of the original criterion, and a newer criterion avoids it.
:::
